PAGE 1. WHAT IS HERE

This is your booklet. Here you write down your goals, your thoughts after sessions and a summary of what you have learned. Nobody checks whether your answers are "right". Write the way that suits you: in words, in a drawing or with the help of a scale.

PAGE 2. QUICK GUIDE

Competence

This is everything you can do, know and how you approach things, all together. It is not a grade, but a description of what you are already able to do.

Example for ages 11–14: "I can ask for help when I am scared." This is a skill and confidence together.

Example for ages 15–18: "I can explain my opinion to the team and calmly accept a different one." This is communication, cooperation and attitude towards people.

Learning goal

One concrete thing you want to learn or do during the session. A good goal answers three questions:

  • What do I want to do?
  • How will I know I have achieved it?
  • What small step can I take today?

Example for ages 11–14: "Today I will push off from the shore by myself. I will know it worked if the boat moves without my help. First step: I will ask to be shown how to hold the paddle."

Example for ages 15–18: "Today I will sail one tack without prompts from the instructor. Criterion: I set the course myself and correct it when the wind changes."

A poor goal (not an example to follow): "Learn to sail." It is hard to check because there is no criterion.

Reflection

Calmly thinking over what happened. It is not an exam or a test. It is enough to answer three questions honestly:

  • What happened?
  • What did I feel and why?
  • What will I do the same or differently next time?

Example for ages 11–14: "What happened: the boat tilted. What I felt: I was scared. Next time: I will ask the instructor to explain once more before turning."

Example for ages 15–18: "What happened: the wind changed and we got the course wrong. What I felt: at first I was angry, then I realised the plan should have been checked. Next time: I will check the forecast before going out."

Learning outcome

Your own wording of what you have learned. Start like this: "I learned to..." or "Now I can...". It is important to add the situation where it happened and what changed.

Example for ages 11–14: "I learned to ask for help when I am scared on the water, and now I am not afraid to say so."

Example for ages 15–18: "I learned to make decisions on the boat and explain them to the team, because before I stayed silent even when I disagreed."

Youthpass

An official document of the European Union that recognises participation in projects as a learning experience. It records the competences you have developed. You can get it if you want. It is not compulsory.

PAGE 3. EIGHT COMPETENCES IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

In one session choose 1–3 competences that fit best. You can skip the others.

1. Multilingual

Understanding and explaining thoughts in different languages, including a foreign one.

On the water: you understand the instructor's command even though they speak English.

Question for yourself: "What did I understand from the instructor or another person during the session?"

2. Personal, social and learning to learn

Learning independently, working in a team, managing emotions.

On the water: you did not panic when the boat tilted, or you asked for

help.

Question for yourself: "What helped me not to give up when it was hard?"

3. Citizenship

Taking a responsible attitude towards shared tasks, people and nature.

On the water: you checked the straps, put the equipment back in its place,

respected the rules of the water body.

Question for yourself: "What did I do to make it good not only for me?"

4. Entrepreneurship

Having ideas, taking initiative, planning and seeing a task through.

On the water: you suggested a new route or plan for the crew and followed

it through.

Question for yourself: "What idea did I suggest and what came of it?"

5. Cultural awareness and expression

Expressing yourself through creativity.

On the water: you came up with a name for the crew, made a video or a drawing about the session.

Question for yourself: "How did I show what mattered to me?"

6. Digital

Using gadgets, forecasts and sources safely and consciously.

On the water: you checked the wind forecast, filmed a video for the report, found

the right information.

Question for yourself: "What information did I check before going out?"

7. Mathematical and STEM

Thinking logically, understanding how wind, water and equipment work.

On the water: you worked out where the wind blows from, or understood why the boat

turns this way and not another.

Question for yourself: "What did I understand about the wind or the equipment?"

8. Literacy

Speaking and writing clearly, understanding instructions, choosing words for different people.

On the water: you read the safety rules, explained a command so that

everyone understood it.

Question for yourself: "How did I explain my thought so that others understood?"
