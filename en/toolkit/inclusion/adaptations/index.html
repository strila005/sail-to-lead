1.1 Recommendations on instructions, pace, difficulty and roles

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/opleidingen/leren-varen/zeilen/waar…

Modular learning at your own pace

  • No rigid deadlines: Training in certified clubs and sailing schools is built in modules. Instead of one final exam for the whole group, the instructor records the mastery of individual elements (steering a course, trimming sails, turns, mooring) in a progress card.
  • Individual pace: If a particular manoeuvre causes difficulty or uncertainty, the participant practises it as long as needed, without group pressure, and moves on to the next module only after the skill is secure.
  • Adapting pace and boarding: At locations for people with disabilities, the pace of instruction is adjusted to the participant's physical and cognitive abilities — hoists are used for safe boarding, an instructor accompanies them on board, and there is a gradual move from shared steering to a fully independent outing on the water.

Grading difficulty by levels

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/recreatie/zeilen

How difficulty is distributed (from beginner to advanced level)

  • Level 1 (Beginner): The first acquaintance with the boat and the wind. Sessions take place in sheltered, calm water in light wind (up to force 3 on the Beaufort scale). The participant learns to hold a course, make basic turns and understand where the wind is coming from.
  • Level 2 (Basic independent level): Consolidating the main manoeuvres, beating against the wind, approaching a jetty or buoy, practising what to do when a dinghy capsizes and applying the basic rules for avoiding collisions on the water.
  • Level 3 (Advanced level): Confident handling of the boat in fresher wind and in busier traffic, precise sail trim, manoeuvring in confined space and making tactical decisions independently.
  • Level 4 (High / skipper level): Independent sailing in difficult weather (in wind up to force 5 on the Beaufort scale), finding one's way on open water and the ability to take responsibility for the safety of other people on board.

Distribution of roles in an inclusive crew

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/wedstrijdsport/zeilen/aangepast-wed…

How roles are distributed in the crew (Unified Sailing format)

  • Sailor with a disability: A full member of the team with an active role on board — works the tiller or the sheets according to their skills and level of training.
  • Sailor without a disability: Acts not as a "passenger" and not only as a safety back-up, but as a crew partner in the same boat. They help with navigation and orientation on the course, prompt the sequence of actions during manoeuvres and share the workload so that the sailor has the maximum independence.
  • Team interaction: Tasks in the boat are clearly divided between helmsman and sheet-hand, which makes it possible to find a manageable area of responsibility for each participant without losing control dynamics.

Training instructors and a safe environment

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/kennisbank/kennisbank-voor-verenigi…

Key principles of training and safety in clubs

  • Qualified mentors: The federation gives clubs methodological support for training instructors and coaches. The main emphasis is on teaching skills: the ability to give clear, calm instructions on the shore and on the water, to reduce the difficulty of tasks in time when the weather changes and to keep beginners motivated.
  • A psychologically and socially safe climate: The recommendations cover not only technical safety (life jackets, motorboat support) but also the atmosphere in the club — no pressure, the right to make mistakes while learning and respect for each participant's comfort limits.
  • Club protection standards: Clubs are advised to have a trusted contact person, transparent rules of conduct in the harbour area and on the water, and vetting of the instructor team, in order to create a predictable environment for children, beginners and people with disabilities.

1.2 Examples for beginners and participants with different levels of confidence

Step-by-step theoretical and practical preparation to reduce anxiety before going out on the water

Sources: Trainingen en opleidingen voor op het water, een overzicht · Zeilen – leer varen, diploma's, verzekeringen

A specialised Dutch system: CWO (Commissie Watersport Opleidingen) and NWD (Nederlandse Watersport Diploma's) — national certification systems for water sports schools and clubs in the Netherlands.

How it works for participants with different levels of confidence

  • Preparation on the shore before the first outing: Webinars and short theory modules introduce beginners to the basic rules for avoiding collisions, the wind forecast and the parts of the boat before they go out on the water, so that participants go out better prepared and more confident.
  • Step-by-step progress cards: Instead of the stress of one big exam, the participant sees a list of small, achievable micro-skills (for example, "can ease the sheet in a gust" or "can approach a jetty safely"). This gives a sense of control over the situation even to very cautious beginners.

A low entry threshold for children and a first introduction without pressure

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/nieuws/optimist-on-tour-brengt-wate…

How it works in practice

  • Short introductory sessions in a familiar environment: The travelling programme Optimist on Tour brings Optimist sailing boats, SUP boards and canoes directly to city harbours and local water bodies across the country for children up to the age of 12.
  • Focus on motor skills and confidence: Sessions are built as free or low-threshold trial outings under constant supervision of instructors on the water, where the main goal is not technical perfection but a positive first experience and overcoming fear of water.
  • Individual adaptation for children with disabilities: The tour includes separate adapted support, where the difficulty and the time spent on the water are matched to the comfort level of the individual child.

A gentle transition from learning to team practice

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/kenniscentrum/wedstrijdsport/beginn…

How it works for beginners with different levels of readiness

  • Taking part without your own boat or board: Beginners do not need to buy equipment straight away — clubs provide club boats and boards for rent or use during training.
  • An experienced crew: If a beginner is not yet ready to go out on the water independently, they can join the crew of an experienced skipper as a deckhand/sheet-hand, gradually picking up experience in a safe role.

Specialised Dutch programmes for participants with low starting confidence and disabilities

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/opleidingen/leren-varen/zeilen/waar… · watersportverbond.nl/wedstrijdsport/zeilen/aangepast-wed…

Specialised Dutch initiatives and their approach

  • Sailability Nederland (club branches, including in Grou, Rotterdam, Sneek and Zeewolde): The organisation uses stable keelboats of the Hansa class (Hansa 2.3, Hansa 303 Wide, Hansa Liberty). Thanks to the heavy keel these boats do not capsize, and the participant sits low inside the hull. This radically lowers anxiety in beginners, in people with musculoskeletal impairments and in those who are afraid of falling into the water. You can start two together with an instructor and later go out independently under the supervision of a shore post.
  • SailWise: A specialised Dutch organisation that runs multi-day sailing camps and outings on adapted boats and dinghies, where instructors individually adjust the load and level of independence for participants with physical, sensory or intellectual impairments.
  • Plons (Rotterdam) and the Unified Sailing format: Programmes for people with intellectual disabilities, where confidence is built through a stable pair in the boat (Unified sporter + Unified partner) and a clear, repeatable division of simple actions on board.

Choosing equipment for gradually growing confidence in windsurfing

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/recreatie/windsurfen

Practical solutions for different levels of training

  • Maximum stability at the start: For unsure beginners, wide high-volume boards (the surfer's own weight + 100–150 litres) and light training sails (3.5–4.5 m² for lighter participants or 4.0–5.5 m² for adults) are used. This makes it easy to keep balance and to raise the sail from the water without tiring quickly.
  • Learning in uniform age groups: Separate programmes for children from the age of 6 (with a special lightened children's sail) and teenagers aged 10–14 help beginners feel more comfortable among peers who progress at the same pace.

1.3 Ways of meaningful participation without going out on the water

A shore-based water laboratory and research games

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/nieuws/optimist-on-tour-brengt-wate…

How children are involved without going out on the water

  • The role of a young researcher instead of a compulsory outing in a boat: As part of the travelling children's programme Optimist on Tour (for children up to 12), a water laboratory is set up on the shore. If a child is afraid of the water or does not yet want to get into a boat, they do not stay on the sidelines but take part in science-and-play activities on land.
  • Experiments with water and wind: Children carry out practical experiments with water, study its quality and water ecology, and explore in a hands-on way how an air stream acts on a sail.
  • Adaptation for children with disabilities: The laboratory and the shore area are designed so that children with different physical or sensory needs can fully interact with the water and the team at a comfortable and safe pace.

Virtual regattas and training in the E-sailing format (Watersport Academy)

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/kenniscentrum/wedstrijdsport/beginn… · watersportverbond.nl/kenniscentrum/wedstrijdsport/wedstr…

How children are involved without going out on the water

  • Full races on the shore (zeilen zonder het water op te gaan): E-sailing is presented by the federation as an equal way of doing sailing and taking part in regattas without going out on the water (in winter, in stormy weather or for children who are not yet ready for real water).
  • Game mechanics of learning: Through the simulator children, in a familiar digital format, master what usually seems difficult in theory: determining wind direction, trimming sails, sailing a course and the rules for avoiding collisions between boats on the water.
  • Team online regattas with a ranking: Clubs organise shared races on screens for children, with results recorded in the SailRanks system, which keeps the excitement and competitive spirit without risk on the water.

Interactive shore practice, a progress card and interaction with the club

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/nieuws/trainingen-en-opleidingen-vo… · watersportverbond.nl/kennisbank/kennisbank-voor-verenigi… · watersportverbond.nl/kennisbank/kennisbank-voor-verenigi…

How children are involved without going out on the water

  • Visualising success on land: In training programmes under the CWO and NWD standards, a large part of the basic skills is practised on the shore — tying the main knots, naming the sides of the boat, rigging a sailing boat, practising manoeuvres on dry trainers or models. Ticking these items off in the progress card gives the child a sense of real forward movement.
  • Mutual help on the shore in a safe environment: The recommendations for clubs stress creating an atmosphere where every child feels needed regardless of whether they went out on the water today. Children work in pairs on the shore: they help peers prepare the boat for launch, check equipment and meet the crews at the jetty after the finish.

1.4 Concrete examples for sailing and windsurfing

Sailing: adapted boat classes and technical solutions

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/wedstrijdsport/zeilen/aangepast-wed… · watersportverbond.nl/opleidingen/leren-varen/zeilen/waar…

Concrete examples in practice

  • Hansa class keelboats (Hansa 2.3, Hansa 303 Wide, Hansa Liberty): Single-handed and two-person sailing boats designed for maximum stability. The heavy keel does not let the boat capsize even in strong gusts, and the low seating inside the cockpit creates a sense of security.
  • Servo electric steering: Special modifications of Hansa class boats fitted with servo drives. They allow participants with severe musculoskeletal impairments to steer and handle the sails with a joystick or buttons without physical strain on the arms.
  • Paralympic and team keelboat classes (2.4mR and Sonar): The single-handed 2.4mR (where the sailor sits low in the hull and all the lines for sail adjustment are brought right to the hands) and the three-person keelboat Sonar for well-coordinated crew work.

Sailing: specialised training programmes and forms of participation

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/opleidingen/leren-varen/zeilen/waar… · watersportverbond.nl/recreatie/zeilen · watersportverbond.nl/nieuws/optimist-on-tour-brengt-wate… · watersportverbond.nl/kenniscentrum/wedstrijdsport/beginn…

Concrete examples in practice

  • Sailability and SailWise: The Sailability programme provides regular training at yacht clubs on Hansa class boats (starting with an outing for two with an instructor and gradually moving to independent steering), and the SailWise organisation runs multi-day sailing camps and outings on adapted boats and dinghies.
  • Unified Sailing: Kanozeilen offers an adapted sailing canoe/kayak as a light and manoeuvrable alternative to a classic dinghy. The Plons initiative (in Rotterdam) and the Unified Sailing format involve people with intellectual disabilities by working in a pair (Unified sporter + Unified partner in one boat).
  • The children's Optimist class and the Niveau 1–4 grading: For children up to the age of 12 the basic boat is the single-handed Optimist dinghy (including within the Optimist on Tour programme). Further training follows 4 levels of difficulty: from basic steering in wind up to force 3 (Niveau 1) to confident independent sailing in wind up to force 5 with responsibility for passengers on board (Niveau 4).

Windsurfing: technical parameters for choosing equipment for beginners

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/recreatie/windsurfen

Concrete examples in practice

  • Calculating the volume of a training board: For beginners a wide, maximally stable, high-volume board is chosen using the formula the surfer's own weight + 100–150 litres. Such reserve buoyancy makes it possible to keep balance on the water with confidence and to raise the sail by the start rope without falling.
  • Limiting the area of the training sail: So that a beginner does not tire and can easily control the pull, light sails of reduced area are used: 3.5–4.5 m² for lighter participants and 4.0–5.5 m² for adult beginners.

Windsurfing: age groups, a modular programme and first competitions

Sources: watersportverbond.nl/recreatie/windsurfen · watersportverbond.nl/kenniscentrum/wedstrijdsport/beginn…

Concrete examples in practice

  • Programme for children from the age of 6: Separate training groups for the youngest, using special lightened children's equipment (ultra-light sails and short masts sized for the child).
  • Teenage groups (10–14 years): Sessions in an age group of peers, where they practise speed control, tacking and gybing, and the skills of safe passing on the water.
  • Basic and optional modules: Learning in windsurfing is built in modules: after completing the compulsory basic modules (starting, sailing on different courses relative to the wind, returning to the starting point) a participant can choose a specialised optional module — for example, competitive windsurfing.
