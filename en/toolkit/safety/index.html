What this pack gives you

A safety and safeguarding checklist, a working risk assessment, the GO/HOLD/NO-GO procedure, a clear briefing for young people and templates for records.

What it does not replace

Local permits, the emergency plan of a specific location, the operator's rules, manufacturer's instructions, the instructor's qualifications and the decisions of the emergency services.

Principle

A safety precondition that is not met means HOLD or NO-GO, not an attempt to "somehow run" the session.

1. Before the event: responsibility, children and location

1.1 Assigning roles and the right to stop a session

FunctionResponsibilityMust be defined
Site manager / organiserApproves the local plan, resources, and the insurance and organisational arrangements; can cancel a session.Deputy, contact, escalation procedure.
Qualified instructorAssesses conditions, the readiness of the group and the equipment; makes the GO/HOLD/NO-GO decision for a specific activity.Competence for the age of the group, the discipline and the type of boat.
Rescue coverReady to help before the group goes out; it is not merely "a boat on the water".Crew, means of communication, ability to lift a person out of the water, fallback plan.
Shore supervision and headcountCalls the roll, keeps the log of departures/returns and the handover between adults.Who supervises the rest of the group during a rescue.
First aid / safeguardingOrganises access to help, medication according to the plan and safe reporting of worrying behaviour.A trusted person and an alternative to the direct leader.
The right to stop a session is unconditional. Nobody may demand to go out against the instructor's decision that it is dangerous. Leaders without the appropriate training do not replace the instructor, and minor helpers do not bear independent responsibility for the group.

1.2 Participants, parents, health and accessibility

  • Before the camp collect up-to-date contacts of parents/guardians, a backup contact, the persons authorised to collect the child and an agreed procedure for early departure.
  • Obtain by a protected method only the data needed for safety: health, allergies, prescribed medication, swimming skills, open-water experience, special needs and the language for explanations.
  • Medication is handed over only according to an agreed plan; the organiser does not change treatment on their own. Emergency medication must be available to designated persons.
  • The instructor admits a participant on the basis of age, experience, wellbeing, understanding of commands and the conditions of the specific session. A claim of being able to swim is checked in a safe way, not in open water without a plan.
  • Non-swimmers, participants with additional needs or those who do not understand the commands need stronger supervision, a suitable buoyancy aid and a separately agreed plan. If these are not in place, there is no going out on the water.
Psychological safety. A child has the right to speak about fear, pain, cold, tiredness, not understanding a command or unsafe behaviour, and to decline an exercise. Such a report is not in itself a breach of discipline.

1.3 Safeguarding, privacy and data

  • Set a clear code of conduct for adults and children, a procedure for reporting bullying, violence or inappropriate touching, and at least one trusted person outside the direct line of supervision.
  • Provide for privacy during changing and personal care, safe accommodation and night supervision if there is an overnight stay.
  • Medical and contact data are available only to those who need them for safety. Define the purpose of collection, the place of storage, the deletion date and emergency access. Consent for photos/publications is obtained separately.

1.4 Choosing and confirming a location

Choose not "a country in general" but a specific operator, water area and session format. Approvals are needed for a children's activity and, if necessary, an overnight stay; rules for the jetty, boarding, emergency exits, shelter, access for medics, access to the first-aid kit and AED. For beginners, preference is given to a sheltered area with controlled traffic, an accessible shore and a real possibility of quick help.

Shipping lanes, locks, bridges, currents, tides, large open water, heavy other traffic, water pollution and dangerous entry/exit points require a separate assessment. Permission for a sailing session does not automatically mean permission to swim.

2. Before and during every session

  1. Set limits. The instructor defines in writing, for the type of boat and the level of the group, the permitted wind and gusts, waves, visibility, water and air temperature, duration, distance from the shore, area and time of return.
  2. Check local information. Before the start and during the session, check the forecast, official warnings, actual conditions, the state of the water, shipping and other local restrictions.
  3. Match the group to the resources. The number of children, boats, instructors and rescue crews must allow help in several simultaneous capsizes while still supervising the other children.
  4. Check equipment and PPE. Before issuing, inspect boats, windsurf kits, life jackets, communication, rescue equipment and clothing. Faulty equipment is isolated and not returned to use until it is cleared again.
  5. Give a short briefing through action. Explain in clear language the limits, the route, signals, stopping, returning, safe handling of the boom/lines, what to do in a capsize and the assembly point. Ask participants to repeat or show the key commands.
  6. Record GO/HOLD/NO-GO. If there is no roll call, supervision, communication, rescue readiness, clear commands or conditions within limits, it is HOLD or NO-GO. Resume only after the cause is removed and a new decision is made.
  7. Keep a headcount until the handover is complete. Before going out, when changing boat/place and after returning, adults check each child. The log is closed only after a personally confirmed handover to the responsible adult.

2.1 Behaviour and buoyancy aids

Participants and adults wear a properly fastened life jacket approved by the instructor in an open boat, on the jetty, during boarding and landing and in marked dangerous shore areas. The jacket is chosen by weight, size, skills, clothing and type of activity; a wetsuit or impact vest is not an automatic replacement.

The following are prohibited: going out on the water on one's own initiative, taking off the life jacket, dangerous jokes, pushing, jumping or swimming without separate permission, deliberate capsizing outside an agreed exercise, leaving the group, alcohol and drugs. Clothing, closed shoes, drinking water, sun protection and a dry change of clothes are chosen to suit the conditions.

2.2 Conditions for a mandatory stop

Start returning early when there is a thunderstorm threat, limits are exceeded, visibility, communication or rescue cover is lost, the water is polluted, a headcount discrepancy cannot be explained, or there is a breakdown or a participant's condition worsens. Do not wait for the scheduled review of the decision.

3. Incidents, safeguarding and closing the day

SituationImmediate procedure
Capsize / person in the waterGive the signal, keep eye contact, point to where the person is. The trained crew starts the rescue, another adult supervises the rest of the group. People take priority over property.
A child or participant cannot be seenStop the session immediately, establish the last place and time, carry out an urgent roll call. If the person may have entered the water, call for help without delay according to the local plan.
Injury or deterioration of conditionStop participation, give help within your training, call the emergency services if urgently needed, follow the dispatcher's instructions. Arrange for medics to be met and for the group to be supervised.
Loss of communication / breakdownUse the agreed backup signal and return plan. Do not continue the programme without the necessary supervision and communication.
Breach of rulesFirst stop the dangerous action and safely hand the participant over to a specific adult. Only then find out the reason, hear the explanation, inform the parents and decide on a proportionate response.

3.1 Suspension and the safe return of a child

A repeated or serious breach may lead to a repeat briefing, temporary suspension, refusal of participation or early end of the camp. In case of immediate danger, action may be immediate. Fear, feeling unwell, reporting a danger or not understanding a command are not treated as a disciplinary breach.

The parents/guardians are informed, after which the child is handed over to them or to a person authorised in advance, with confirmation of the handover. Until then the child remains under supervision. A minor is never sent home alone.

3.2 Closing the day

  • The roll call is closed: the whereabouts of every child are known.
  • The return of people, boats and equipment is confirmed; the logs are closed.
  • Defects, dangerous situations, incidents and open actions are entered in the records.
  • If there is an overnight stay, night supervision is handed over to the designated persons.
  • The team briefly analyses what happened and updates the risks before the next session.

4. Country cards and the register of local sources

This is not a handbook of laws. For each actual location the team fills in a card with official and local sources, the date of checking and the status. Anything unconfirmed is not presented as established fact.
