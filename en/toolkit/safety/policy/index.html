Handling of equipment, craft, technical aids and life jackets

OESP-2026 • Windsurfing and training sailing boats • Five-day event

APPROVED

1 General provisions

1.1. This policy sets out the procedure for safe work at the base, issuing and using equipment, going out on the water, returning, technical maintenance and action in case of danger. It applies to staff, instructors, participants, volunteers and authorised visitors, in the relevant part.

1.2. The document is an adapted internal policy of OESP-2026. It is not an official document of KNRM or Reddingsbrigade Nederland, does not certify regulatory compliance and comes into force for an event once the local conditions are filled in and it is approved by the organiser.

1.3. Before the event begins, the organiser agrees the requirements of the local authorities and the owner of the base, the manufacturers' instructions, the response plan, the limits of the water area, the permitted conditions and the admission procedure. This text does not set universal numerical weather limits or provision standards.

1.4. Only equipment that is serviceable, inspected and approved for the specific participant and activity may be used. If the mandatory checks assigned to the responsible persons or the agreed conditions are missing, going out is not permitted.

1.5. Everyone must report a danger immediately and stop their own unsafe action. The session may be resumed only by the designated person after a new assessment. Returning equipment does not replace the personal accounting of people.

2 Responsible persons and order at the base

Distribution of duties

2.1. The event manager approves the local conditions, appoints the responsible persons and the person who makes the GO/HOLD/NO-GO decision, ensures the response plan and restricts activity if readiness is insufficient.

2.2. The person responsible for equipment and gear keeps the register, organises inspections, issuing and returning, isolates defective equipment and oversees repairs. Carrying out a repair is not in itself clearance for use.

2.3. The instructor checks that the equipment suits the participant, gives the briefing and supervises the group, PPE and the limits of sessions. The person responsible for rescue provision confirms the readiness of people, craft and communication for the agreed plan.

2.4. The shore registrar records every departure and return, the make-up of groups and the expected time. The participant follows commands, inspects the issued equipment together with the instructor and reports tiredness, cold, feeling unwell and faults.

Safety of the shore area

2.5. The areas for storage, rigging, walkways, launching, returning, repairs and charging must be defined and clearly marked. Walkways, emergency exits and access to the first-aid kit, rescue and fire-fighting equipment are kept clear.

2.6. Do not run on slippery decking, jump from the jetty, push each other or enter the water without permission. Wear suitable footwear. Do not allow children and visitors into work areas unsupervised.

2.7. Carry long, heavy and sail items on command and with enough people. Before moving, check the route, the wind and the space overhead. Keep masts away from power lines; keep fingers and feet out of pinch points.

2.8. Place boards, boats, sails and masts on the designated supports and secure them against gusts, falling and rolling away. Do not leave a sail unattended. Do not sit on fragile parts or drag equipment across surfaces that damage it.

2.9. Only authorised persons may work with tools, in a designated area. Use the necessary protective equipment and clear away sharp objects, ropes and waste. Disconnect faulty power tools and hand them to the responsible person.

2.10. Alcohol, drugs and any state that impairs the ability to act safely are incompatible with operating, repairing and taking part on the water. Report any limitation of fitness to work to the manager before starting.

3 Equipment and craft

Recording and inspection

3.1. Every kit has an ID. It is issued after inspection and linked to a participant or group. Check completeness, the hull, fastenings, moving parts and conformity with the manufacturer's instructions. After an impact, overload or suspected damage, a repeat inspection is needed.

3.2. Enter OK, Defect or N/A for each applicable item; explain any N/A. An empty item means the check is not confirmed. It is forbidden to hide defects, change settings without approval or swap incompatible parts.

Windsurfing

3.3. Before use, check the board, the mast and its joints, the boom and its locks, the sail, the mast base and joint, the fin, the daggerboard if present, the uphaul rope and fastenings. The sail size and kit are chosen by the instructor taking into account the participant and conditions.

3.4. Rig and carry the equipment in the designated area, controlling the sail and the sharp edges of the fin. Do not stand in the path of the mast and boom and do not point them at people. Launch a kit only with the instructor's permission, when the area ahead is clear.

3.5. Use the harness and foot straps only after training in releasing from them and an assessment of the entanglement risk. A leash is not a mandatory universal addition to a windsurfer: its use, type and release are determined by the manufacturer and a separate assessment of conditions. Home-made body tethers are prohibited.

Training sailing boats

3.6. Check the hull, hatches and buoyancy compartments, bungs, mast, boom, standing rigging if present, sheets, blocks, cleats, sails, rudder, tiller, daggerboard and the means of bailing and towing provided by the manufacturer. Do not exceed the permitted load.

3.7. Launch and recover on command, controlling the trolley, the slope, the edges of the hull and pinch points. Board after the boat is stable, without jumping and without shifting the weight of the whole crew to one side.

3.8. Before going out, explain the danger of being hit by the boom, the procedure for changing places, tacking and what to do in a capsize. Keep the head and limbs out of the path of the rigging. Do not wrap sheets or other ropes around the hands, neck or body.

Prohibition of use

3.9. Remove a faulty kit from issue, mark it and move it to quarantine. Record the defect ID, the responsible person and the deadline. It may be returned to use only after the cause is eliminated, it is re-inspected and an authorised person has recorded permission.

4 Life jackets and other personal protective equipment

Choosing the appropriate equipment

4.1. In this policy the word "life jacket" covers various buoyancy aids. They are not interchangeable. A buoyancy aid helps an active person stay afloat but is not designed to turn an unconscious person face up. The ability of a lifejacket to support the airway depends on its design and the conditions [1, 2].

4.2. The organiser determines the type needed on the basis of a risk assessment: the type of activity, swimming ability, age, weight and size, clothing, temperature, likelihood of capsizing and availability of help. For children, non-swimmers and persons with additional needs, separately agreed selection and support are required.

4.3. Do not treat a wetsuit or impact vest as an automatic substitute for a buoyancy aid. Confirm suitability from the marking and the instructions for this specific model. Do not assign an inflatable jacket to sessions with frequent immersion or a risk of entrapment without checking its suitability for such use.

Inspection and putting on

4.4. Before every issue, check the ID, size, marking, fabric, seams, filling or shell, straps, zips, buckles and other provided parts. For inflatable models also check the indicators, cylinder, firing mechanism and service dates according to the manufacturer's instructions [1, 2].

4.5. Put it on over the actual clothing. Fasten and adjust all straps provided, including crotch straps where there are any. Remove twisted and loosely hanging straps. The instructor checks the fit using the manufacturer's method: the jacket must not ride up or hinder movement and breathing [1, 2].

4.6. For this event the following rule is set: the approved buoyancy aid is worn fastened while on a craft and on the water. On jetties, slipways and by an unprotected water edge it is worn in zones defined by the local risk assessment. The boundaries of these zones are marked before work begins.

4.7. Do not pass a life jacket to another person without refitting it. Do not use it as a seat, a cushion or a place for tools. Do not lift a person by the jacket unless its design and instructions provide for such use.

Defects and care

4.8. Do not issue a jacket that is damaged, the wrong size, contaminated with hazardous substances or of unconfirmed readiness. After activation, withdraw an inflatable jacket until it has been restored and checked in accordance with the manufacturer.

4.9. After use, clean, rinse if needed and dry it according to the instructions. Store in a ventilated place without compression, direct heat or contact with fuel. Carry out inspections, servicing and replacement according to the manufacturer's requirements and applicable local rules [1, 2].

5 Technical and auxiliary equipment

Motor and rescue boats, where present

5.1. Only a designated operator with the training and authorisations needed for the place and type of vessel may operate. Having a boat does not mean rescue provision is ready without a crew, communication, necessary equipment and an agreed procedure.

5.2. Before work, check the hull and buoyancy, the engine, steering, the fuel or charge reserve, the means of stopping, communication and equipment against an approved list. Make sure there are no people, ropes or obstacles near the engine.

5.3. If an emergency engine cut-off cord is fitted, the operator uses it as intended, checks it works before the session and does not lengthen it on their own. The engine is stopped before the operator changes. The cord must not interfere with the controls [3].

5.4. If there is a risk of contact between a person in the water and the propeller, stop the engine. When recovering someone from the water, do not rely on neutral gear alone. Rule out accidental starting while people are near the propeller; follow the procedure of the specific boat [4].

5.5. Towing is carried out by a trained crew only in an agreed way through suitable attachment points. Do not stand in loops or in the line of possible snap-back of a taut rope. Do not attach a tow to the body; towing with people is governed by a separate procedure.

Fuel and batteries

5.6. Refuel in the designated place with the engine off, according to the instructions. Remove ignition sources, ensure ventilation and spill-response equipment. Store fuel in the designated container; do not combine refuelling with participants boarding.

5.7. Charge batteries with compatible, serviceable equipment in a dry area according to the instructions. Protect connectors from water and short circuits. Do not use or charge a damaged, overheated, deformed or wet battery; act according to the fire safety plan and the manufacturer's recommendations.

Radio communication and electrical equipment

5.8. Before going out, check the charge, water protection and communication between the shore, the instructor and the rescue provision. Define call signs, the working and emergency calling procedure and a backup device. Use channels and radios in accordance with local requirements; no universal channel number is set here.

5.9. Secure radios without dangerous loops around the neck. Protect the cables of shore equipment from water, damage and tripping. Do not carry out repairs while live, do not touch electrical appliances with wet hands and do not use damaged extension leads.

6 Admission and rules of conduct on the water

Before going out

6.1. The decision-maker confirms the zone map, the current risk assessment, the forecast and actual conditions, the serviceability of equipment, the selection of PPE and the readiness of instructors, communication and rescue provision. A GO record applies only to the stated activity, group, zone and period.

6.2. HOLD means a pause until the causes are eliminated and a new decision is made. NO-GO prohibits the stated activity. The absence of a record or signature is not permission. Group sizes, provision and limits of conditions are defined before going out and are not exceeded.

6.3. The instructor gives a briefing: limits and dangerous places, launching and returning, signals, rules for passing, what to do after a fall, a capsize, equipment failure and loss of contact. Practical skills are checked in controlled conditions before they are performed independently.

6.4. The registrar records the outing ID, the craft, the ID of each person, the number, the zone, the time of departure and the expected return. Every repeat outing is recorded separately. The participant tells the instructor about any limitation of wellbeing before the start.

During the session

6.5. Keep to the designated zone and the instructor's commands, and maintain the required visual or radio contact. Do not change group, route, crew or kit on your own. Do not enter bathing areas, shipping lanes or other prohibited areas.

6.6. Keep watching the surroundings. Choose speed and manoeuvres that allow collisions to be avoided. Follow the applicable local rules of navigation and passing explained at the briefing. Do not race or deliberately come dangerously close outside an approved session.

6.7. Do not wrap ropes around the body, stand in loops or keep limbs between moving parts. A knife or other emergency release device is used only by a trained person, if it is provided for by the risk assessment and is securely fixed.

6.8. After a fall, capsize or breakdown, give the agreed signal and follow the trained procedure. Stay in contact with the craft unless this creates a risk of entrapment, impact or other immediate danger. Do not start an unagreed swim to the shore and do not chase equipment at risk to yourself.

When a session is stopped

6.9. Report immediately and begin the agreed procedure for stopping or returning when conditions go beyond the approved limits, there is a thunderstorm threat, visibility, communication or rescue readiness is lost, there is drift outside the zone, a breakdown, an injury, severe cold or exhaustion. Do not wait for the scheduled review of the decision.

7 Dangerous events and finishing work

Responding to danger

7.1. A witness gives the agreed signal and tells the instructor or the shore post: what happened, the place and landmarks, the number of people, the visible danger and the help needed. The shore post activates the approved response plan and calls local help under its terms.

7.2. Rescue on the water is carried out by trained personnel within their abilities. Do not create a second casualty. Direct other participants to a safe area, stop traffic at the scene and ensure access for help. Medical action is carried out according to training and the dispatcher's instructions.

7.3. In the event of a failure to return, loss of contact or a discrepancy in the headcount, immediately inform the manager and the person responsible for rescue provision. Check the last place, time, and the IDs of the people and the craft, and act according to the search plan. Reconciling the logs must not delay necessary help.

7.4. In case of fire, dangerous overheating of a battery or a fuel spill, stop work, move people away and inform the responsible person. Disconnect and extinguish only in the safe way provided for by the plan and the equipment instructions. Do not move a dangerous battery with bare hands.

7.5. After an event, preserve the records and damaged equipment, unless this interferes with help or removing the danger. Record the time, circumstances, actions taken and the defect or incident ID. Before sessions resume, review the risks and issue a new decision.

Returning and daily closing

7.6. Return through the agreed zone on command. Take the load off the rig, stabilise the craft, disembark in an orderly way and carry the equipment out. Take off the life jacket after leaving the danger zone and with the instructor's permission.

7.7. Confirm the return of each person by ID and time, and the return of the craft. An equal number at departure and arrival does not prove the same people are present. Treat any unexplained absence of a person under the response procedure, not as an ordinary recording error.

7.8. After use, inspect, clean, dry and store the equipment according to the instructions. Enter defects in the log; do not return faulty items to general storage. Record the remaining fuel, the charge, consumables and repair needs.

7.9. At the end of every day the manager reconciles people, craft, issued property and open actions. After day 5, a final handover is drawn up stating discrepancies, quarantine, responsible persons and deadlines. The handover sheet is signed by both parties.

OESP records

Use the forms: 00 passport; 01 water area map; 02 and 03 inspections; 04 RI&E and action plan; 05 departure and return; 06 GO/HOLD/NO-GO; 07 defects; 08 handover. Keep the IDs of people, equipment, risks and defects the same in all records.
