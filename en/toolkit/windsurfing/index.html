In the process of learning windsurfing, participants will develop self-confidence, independence and responsibility for their own safety and for the proper use of equipment. They will learn to cooperate, support each other and work as a team. Thanks to the practical sessions, participants will be better able to overcome difficulties, adapt to changing conditions on the water, treat mistakes as part of the learning process and assess their own progress and results independently.

A practical module for a leader who runs sessions for a youth group together with a qualified windsurfing instructor. It is based on the RYA Start Windsurfing and Youth Stages 1–2 methodology and the structure of the VDWS basic course [1–4].

Purpose and limits. Purpose: in favourable conditions the participant learns to handle the equipment, stand on the board, raise the sail, move across the wind, stop, turn round and return to a designated area. More complex manoeuvres are introduced only when the participant is ready. This is a sample youth module, not an RYA/VDWS certification course.

1. Learning sequence and criteria for moving on

Method for each stage: a brief explanation → the instructor's demonstration → repetition on land/the simulator → short practice on the water → feedback. The instructor decides on moving on by the demonstrated skill, not by time [1].

StageWhat we practiseReadiness criterion
1. Equipment and windBoard, daggerboard/fin, mast, boom, sail, uphaul rope; wind direction, signals, limits of the area.Names the main parts; shows the wind direction and the stop signal; understands the limits of the water area.
2. Land / simulatorStance, balance, raising the sail (uphaul), neutral V-position, moving to the sailing stance.Performs the sequence with controlled posture, without unsafe movements.
3. First outingSafe entry/exit, raising the sail on the water, controlling the pull, stopping.With the instructor present, raises the sail, sets off and releases the power.
4. Moving and returningShort tacks across the wind; a static 180° turn, returning to a mark.Covers a short distance in both directions and returns to the training area.
5. SteeringChanging direction by tilting the sail; a tack (through the wind).Controls direction and performs a turn in controlled conditions.
6. Development — when readySailing closer/further off the wind, a simple route, an introduction to bearing away and gybing.Completes a route without losing control; the instructor permits the next level of difficulty.

Safety throughout. At all stages: the exercise "let go of the rear hand — release the power", the signal for help, staying with the board and acting according to the rescue plan. Self-rescue is demonstrated by the instructor [1, 5–7].

2. Conditions and organisation of a session

  • Location: a sheltered water area permitted for training with a safe entry; the instructor checks the forecast, offshore wind (risk of being blown away), currents, thunderstorms, visibility and other restrictions. For the first lessons — light, predictable wind; there is no universal threshold for all groups [5, 8].
  • Equipment: wide, stable training boards (with a daggerboard if needed), light sails suited to participants' height and strength, serviceable joints; fitted buoyancy aids, a wetsuit/protection from the cold and footwear according to conditions [1, 4, 7].
  • Before going out: check the equipment, individual needs, signals, the limits of the area and the headcount of participants; the readiness of rescue cover and the emergency plan [5].
  • Roles: the technical instructor selects the equipment, determines permissible conditions, demonstrates technique, and runs the session on the water and decisions about rescue. The leader runs games on land, turns, support, observation and reflection; does not replace the instructor [1, 5].
  • Different pace: the group alternates between short water practice, the simulator, paired observation and a wind diagram. Tasks are differentiated: a lighter sail/shorter distance or more precise steering/an extra turn. Do not compare participants by speed.
  • Inclusion: ask about individual needs, agree on a way of communicating, dignity and voluntary participation; agree adaptations on the water with the instructor. Equal roles are available for those who do not go out on the water.
  • Ecology: do not enter protected zones, observe the rules of the water body, do not leave rubbish and do not disturb animals; take the local ecology group's recommendations into account in every scenario [10].

3. Session scenarios

Guideline: 5 sessions of 75–90 minutes with breaks; further practice is needed to consolidate, as assessed by the instructor. This does not guarantee reaching a certification standard: RYA and VDWS define the scope of the course separately [2–4].

Session 1. Equipment, wind and balance

Educational goalFinding one's way around the equipment; safe behaviour; confidence at first contact.
Target groupBeginners; swimming skills and age admission — according to the training centre's requirements.
Duration and format75 min; land/training simulator (going out on the water is not required).
EquipmentA training board, a sail, a simulator, a flag/wind ribbons, buoyancy aids.
Practice stages10 min — rules, signals and limits; 15 — parts of the equipment; 15 — determining the wind; 25 — raising the sail and the V-position on the simulator; 10 — summary.
Instructor's roleSelecting and checking equipment; demonstrating the uphaul and the basic stance.
Leader's role"Find the wind" exercises, work in pairs, turns, recording progress.
AdaptationsA lighter sail, support on the simulator, step-by-step demonstration.
Alternative planA full simulator module, cards of equipment parts and wind.
ReflectionWhat did I learn to control? What helped me not to be afraid of a mistake?
Progress criteriaRecognises the wind, reproduces the basic stance and signals.

Session 2. First metres on the water

Educational goalTo set off independently and stop safely; to learn to accept help.
Target groupParticipants who have done the exercises of session 1.
Duration and format90 min; short outings in a limited training area.
EquipmentStable boards, light sails, suitable buoyancy aids, marker buoys.
Practice stages10 min — repeating signals; 15 — demonstration on the simulator; 15 — launching, getting on and the uphaul; 35 — moving across the wind in short stretches, releasing the power; 5 — coming ashore; 10 — reflection.
Instructor's roleDemonstration from the water; constant supervision, correcting the stance, controlling distance and return.
Leader's roleHeadcount of participants, turns, support after failures; orientation exercises on the shore.
AdaptationsA distance of 10–20 m only as a training example, the actual one is set by the instructor; shorter attempts, a smaller sail.
Alternative planSimulating the start on the simulator, video analysis of body position.
ReflectionHow did I understand that the sail was pulling? How did I stop?
Progress criteriaRaises the sail, sets off and releases the power on command.

Session 3. Across the wind and turning round

Educational goalControlled movement there and back; patience and mutual help.
Target groupParticipants who start and stop confidently.
Duration and format90 min; simulator + a short water course.
EquipmentStandard training equipment, 2 markers/buoys.
Practice stages10 min — repetition; 20 — a static turn: tilting the sail back, small steps round the mast, a new V-position; 45 — short tacks with a turn; 5 — return; 10 — discussion.
Instructor's roleDemonstrating the turn on land and on the water; watches for drift downwind.
Leader's roleObserver pairs: one notes what worked, the other gives one tip.
AdaptationsA slower turn, fewer turns; for the more experienced — a more precise stop at a mark.
Alternative planThe simulator, modelling the route "start–buoy–shore".
ReflectionWhat changed when I tilted the sail? How did I support another person?
Progress criteriaTwo controlled short tacks and a turn in the training area.

Session 4. Steering and tacking

Educational goalTo change course; to plan an action and correct mistakes.
Target groupParticipants who sail a short distance with a turn.
Duration and format90 min; simulator + water.
EquipmentTraining boards and sails, buoys for a simple S-course.
Practice stages10 min — repetition; 15 — demonstration: mast back → closer to the wind, forward → further from the wind; 15 — practising the tack on the simulator; 40 — the S-course and turning through the bow on the water; 10 — reflection.
Instructor's roleDemonstrating the tack, choosing safe points for turning, correcting technique.
Leader's roleA route diagram on the shore, observation/rotation and short tips.
AdaptationsFirst a slow static turn; for the prepared — a smooth tack while moving.
Alternative planA "board–wind–buoys" model, practising the turn on the simulator.
ReflectionWhat decision helped to turn the board? What did I change after a mistake?
Progress criteriaChanges course and performs a turn through the wind while keeping control of the situation.

Session 5. Route, self-rescue and summary

Educational goalTo combine skills with responsibility for oneself and the group; to assess personal progress.
Target groupParticipants after the basic practice; the difficulty of the route is determined individually.
Duration and format90 min; a short route, a safety demonstration, reflection.
EquipmentTraining equipment, buoys, signalling equipment/the centre's rescue support.
Practice stages10 min — plan and conditions; 45 — the route "start–buoy–change of direction–return"; 15 — the instructor demonstrates self-rescue (depending on conditions — safe practice); 20 — individual summary.
Instructor's roleSets routes according to level; demonstrates the help signal and self-rescue; assesses the decision on further practice.
Leader's roleRecords personal progress, supports feedback, runs the reflection.
AdaptationsAn easier route for a beginner; when ready — sailing closer to the wind, a first attempt at a gybe.
Alternative planSimulating the return plan/signals, going through "what to do if the wind picks up".
ReflectionWhat did I do independently today? When did I ask for help in good time?
Progress criteriaReturns to the area according to the plan; knows the help signal and explains how to act in a problem.

4. Plan B: when practice on the water is not possible

ReasonSolution on landWhat we learn
Gusty/excessive wind, thunderstorm, cold, poor visibilityCancel the outing; simulator: stance, balance and releasing the power; safe carrying of equipment.Risk assessment, technical sequence.
Dangerous waves, currents, restricted access to the water bodyA diagram of wind directions and a short route with buoys on land; "what do I do if…" exercises.Orientation, decision-making.

5. Educational outcome and review

After every session the leader briefly records not only the technical skill but also a behavioural observation. At the end of the series each participant states one skill gained, one situation of mutual help and a next step [9].

Educational outcomeObservable behaviour
Confidence and independenceTries again after a failed attempt; asks for help appropriately.
Responsibility and safetyChecks equipment, keeps to limits and signals, stops an attempt in time.
Mutual helpGives a considerate tip, supports a partner, does not pressure the weaker one.
Reflection and learningNames a specific mistake, a change of actions and their next learning step.
Environmental responsibilityFollows the rules of the water body and treats the environment with care.

Appendix A. Pre-departure checklist

  • Forecast, wind direction, visibility, temperature, local prohibitions and the limits of the area have been checked.
  • A qualified instructor has confirmed the suitability of conditions and the make-up of the group; a rescue plan is ready.
  • Boards, masts, joints, fastenings and sails are serviceable; sizes are chosen individually.
  • Buoyancy aids are fitted; participants know the signals, the rules of movement and safe return.
  • There is a headcount of all participants, duty rota, drinking water, protection from cold/sun.
  • There is a "stop" decision: who stops the session, how to bring everyone back and how to call for help.

6. Expected educational outcomes

After completing the learning module, participants not only master the basic technical skills of windsurfing but also develop personal and social competences:

Educational outcomeBrief description of the expected behaviour
Self-confidenceThe participant dares to try new actions, treats mistakes as part of learning and notices their own progress.
Responsibility for oneself and equipmentFollows safety rules, checks and carefully uses equipment, reports faults and asks for help in good time.
Mutual helpSupports other participants, gives friendly tips, respects different learning paces and works in a pair/team.
Ability to work through difficultiesDoes not stop trying after a failure, analyses a difficult situation, adjusts actions and asks for help when needed.
Ability to assess one's own progressCan name the skills already mastered, identify what still needs practice and set the next personal goal.

7. Reflection questions at the end of a session

1. What new thing did I learn to do today on the board or with the equipment? 2. What went best today and why? 3. What difficulty did I face and how did I try to overcome it? 4. At what moment did I feel more confident than at the start of the session?

5. How did I look after my own safety and equipment today? 6. Whom did I help today or from whom did I receive support? 7. What do I want to practise next time to see my progress?

PART II. RESOURCES FOR SAILING: POLAND, SLOVAKIA AND UKRAINE

I suggest organising the work as a joint international project to create a base of practical resources for Plast and scout leaders in three countries: Poland, Slovakia and Ukraine.

The main goal is not simply to collect links but to create a convenient catalogue of verified places, organisations, services and opportunities that leaders can use to plan camps, hikes, training courses and other activities.

1. Create a working structure

Project coordinator Responsible for deadlines, common standards, communication between countries and the final catalogue. Three national leads Poland, Slovakia, Ukraine. Each collects information, finds local experts and checks sources. Regional researchers Study specific activity locations, transport, infrastructure, rules and opportunities for groups. Editor / verification lead Removes duplicates, monitors the completeness of records, confirmation statuses and the quality of the final material. In a small group one person can take on several roles. If there is no representative of a country, the lead turns to a partner organisation.

4. Organise the work over 4 weeks

Week 1 Preparation and allocation Assign the responsible people, choose regions, create the table, agree the categories and verification criteria. Result: a ready structure and a research plan.

Week 2 Searching for information Collect official sources, local organisations, locations, infrastructure, published rates. Result: a preliminary catalogue for the three countries.

Week 3 Contacts and verification Contact clubs, administrations, instructors and scouts. Clarify prices, restrictions, accessibility and rules. Result: records with confirmed sources and a list of open questions.

Week 4 Editing and presentation Check completeness, produce regional overviews, prepare a map and short recommendations. Result: a ready catalogue for leaders.

6. How to monitor progress

Coordinator's checklist 0 / 6 Those responsible for the three countries assigned Specific regions and activity locations defined A single resource table created Sources and contacts of local partners collected Key terms and prices checked The final catalogue and map prepared I recommend holding one joint meeting a week lasting 30 minutes. Each national lead briefly reports on what was found, checked, problem questions and the help needed.

7. What the final result should be

It is best to prepare two products: An internal working register — a full database with contacts, history of checks, prices, questions and responsible people.

A public catalogue for leaders — a guide structured by country and region with verified resources, practical tips, links and an interactive map.

Google Drive with Google Sheets is well suited to joint work: participants can update a single database without sending different versions of files around. Google Drive can be connected to ChatGPT in order to work directly with the shared tables in future.

One important question: is this a task for an international group of course participants who are to do the research together, or are you organising a long-term project for Plast leaders?

It depends on this whether it is better to divide the work by country or to form mixed international teams with specific tasks.

2. Overview of resources

The Netherlands

The most promising regions: Veluwemeer (Strand Horst, Biddinghuizen) – shallows, safe; Veerse Meer – an enclosed lake without tides; IJsselmeer (Workum, Makkum) – broad sandy shallows.

General conditions: the season is from May to the end of September. Water temperature 15–20°C. Long wetsuits are compulsory. Typical winds: westerly and south-westerly.

Local restrictions: no special permits are needed, entering swimming zones and fairways is strictly prohibited.

Spain

The most promising regions: Mar Menor (Murcia) – a lagoon, large shallows; the Ebro Delta (Trabucador) – a lagoon, no waves; the Bay of Roses (Sant Pere Pescador) – campsites by the water; the Bay of Alcúdia (Mallorca) – a sheltered bay.

General conditions: the season is from May to October. Water 18–26°C. In spring and autumn wetsuits are needed.

Local restrictions: going out on the water in swimming zones is prohibited. Marked channels must always be used. Inland water bodies require a permit (declaración responsable).

Austria

The most promising regions: Neusiedler See (Podersdorf) – a shallow steppe lake (1–1.5 m); Achensee – an alpine lake (cold); the Salzkammergut lakes (Mondsee) – fresh water, close to scout bases.

General conditions: the season is from May to September. Water 18–28°C in the east, colder in the mountains. Wetsuits are compulsory.

Local restrictions: entering reed thickets (protected zones) is strictly prohibited. A visual storm warning system is in operation.
